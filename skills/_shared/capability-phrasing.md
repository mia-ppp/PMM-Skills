# Capability Phrasing

Shared rules for any skill that names what a product does: positioning statements, hero lines, subheads, capability tables, and proof.

## Rules

- **Capabilities are concrete and mechanism-level.** Usually 2 to 4 words. Examples: "auto-drafting invoices", "field-level provenance", "finance-approved process".
- **Describe what the product does, never what the company has.** Bad: "the abusers Stripe already knows." That is an asset the company holds, not something the product does for the buyer.
- **No abstract nouns as capabilities.** Banned as capabilities: visibility, insights, intelligence, protection, platform. Replace each with the mechanism behind it.
- **The engineer test.** Could an engineer point to the feature this phrase describes? If not, rewrite it until they could.

| Abstract | Concrete |
|---|---|
| Better visibility | Line-item payout matching |
| Revenue intelligence | Auto-tagging deal risks |
| Fraud protection | Pre-authorization card checks |

## Hero line archetypes

Pick one archetype per set of hero lines, and name it.

| Archetype | Use when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Super specific who | The audience itself is the differentiator | Canva: "The design tool for people who aren't designers" |
| Problem solver | One pain is sharp and widely felt | Loom: "Loom on. Meetings off." |
| Category differentiator | The category is familiar and stale | The Browser Company: "Get excited about your web browser" |
| How-to | The mechanism is simple and surprising | Uber: "Push a button, get a ride" |
| Feature no one has | One capability is unmatched and easy to grasp | Nokia: "1 phone. 5 cameras." |
| Big promise | The outcome is measurable and credible | Butter: "Increase top-line ARR by 5% by fixing payment failures" |
