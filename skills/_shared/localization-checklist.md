# Localization checklist

Skills that move copy or claims into a new market load this file and check every category below. Label every market rule Sourced, Assumed, or Gap, as defined in `evidence-gaps.md`.

This checklist names what to check. It is not legal advice. A regulatory rule counts as Sourced only when it cites a regulator, statute, or counsel for the target market.

## 1. Currency and units

- **Currency matches the market.** Show prices in the local currency, with the local symbol, code, and placement. Never convert a price without a sourced rate and a date.
- **Tax treatment is stated the local way.** Check whether consumer prices must include sales tax or VAT in the target market.
- **Units match the market.** Metric or imperial, paper sizes, and measurement words.
- **Numbers inside claims stay true after conversion.** A converted figure keeps its original scope and source.

## 2. Date and number formats

- **Date order matches the market.** For example, 03/04/2026 is 4 March in the US and 3 April in the UK. Write the month as a word when the order is ambiguous.
- **Decimal and thousands separators match the market.** For example, 1,000.50 in the US is 1.000,50 in Germany.
- **Time format matches the market.** 12-hour or 24-hour, and the time zone named.
- **Week start and holidays fit the market** when the copy names a day, deadline, or launch date.

## 3. Spelling variant

- **One spelling variant per market.** For example, en-US "optimize, color, organization" and en-GB "optimise, colour, organisation."
- **Product names, legal names, and trademarks never change spelling.** Only the surrounding copy is localized.
- **Idioms and sports metaphors translate or go.** A phrase that works in one market can confuse or offend in another.

## 4. Regulatory and financial-claims language

- **Every license or authorization names its jurisdiction.** "Licensed" alone implies everywhere.
- **Protected words need a license in the target market.** Check terms such as bank, insured, deposit, savings, and investment before using them.
- **Outcome claims need the local qualifier.** Check returns, yields, guarantees, savings figures, and "risk-free" language against the market's advertising and financial-promotion rules.
- **Required disclosures travel with the claim.** Check whether the market requires a risk warning, an issuer name, or a regulator reference next to the claim.
- **Availability claims name where the product is live.** A product live in one market is not live in the next until sourced.

### Fintech note: a claim legal in one jurisdiction may not be legal in another

Financial claims do not travel on their own. A stablecoin claim that is accurate in the US after the GENIUS Act, such as calling a coin a regulated payment stablecoin, is not automatically accurate in the EU. There, stablecoins fall under MiCA's own authorization rules. Treat every regulatory status as specific to one jurisdiction. Label it Gap for the target market until a source for that market confirms it.

## 5. Market-specific banned or sensitive phrases

- **Superlatives and comparisons follow local advertising rules.** Check "#1," "the leading," "the only," and named competitor comparisons.
- **"Free" and "no fees" follow local consumer rules.** Check whether the market requires the conditions to sit next to the claim.
- **Sensitive topics differ by market.** Check references to religion, politics, national symbols, and historical events.
- **Local sensitive words go on the list.** Add any word a local reviewer flags, with the reason, so the next check catches it.
