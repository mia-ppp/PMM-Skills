# Positioning, capability, and hero line examples (v2)

Reference examples curated by Mia. Skills that write positioning, messaging, or hero lines load this file and follow it. Positioning and messaging are separate outputs with separate rules.

## 1. Positioning statement

**Formula:** Positioning = product differentiation (is a) + customer segmentation (for).

Write it as one sentence of 25 words or fewer: "[Product] is a [category + one short differentiating phrase] for [segment]."

| Product | Is a (product differentiation) | For (customer segmentation) |
|---|---|---|
| DuckDuckGo | a search engine that doesn't steal your data | privacy-conscious individuals |
| Canva | web-based design tool with drag-and-drop functionality | non-designers |
| Mercury | tech-first banking with API integrations | startups and tech companies |
| Product Hunt | curated platform showcasing new tech products daily | entrepreneurs, tech enthusiasts, and early adopters |

Rules:
- The statement is one sentence, 25 words or fewer.
- The "is a" slot is the category plus one short differentiating phrase, a single concrete phrase such as "network-matched" or "built into payments." Never a list of capabilities or checkpoints.
- All capabilities, checkpoints, and mechanisms go in the capability table, not the statement.
- The "for" slot is a specific segment, never "everyone" or "businesses."
- Positioning is internal strategy. It is plain and precise, not clever.
- Under the statement, show a derivation trace: which research finding or buyer quote supports each slot, and which slots are assumptions.

Bad to good:

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| Bad (34 words, lists four checkpoints) | Ledgerline is a treasury platform that tracks multi-chain stablecoin balances in real time, reconciles every transfer against the general ledger, screens wallets before payouts, and generates audit-ready reports for finance teams at crypto-native companies. |
| Good (12 words) | Ledgerline is a stablecoin-native treasury tool for finance teams at crypto companies. |

The four checkpoints are not lost. They become rows in the capability table: real-time multi-chain balances, ledger-matched transfers, pre-payout wallet screening, and audit-ready reports.

## 2. Capabilities: the bridge between features and benefits

- **Feature:** the technical aspect of the product.
- **Capability:** the new ability the feature unlocks for the customer. Verb-led and concrete.
- **Benefit:** the state change the capability drives.

| Product | Feature | Capability | Benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loom | Video and screen recording | Record quick demonstration videos to share with your team | Cut down meetings by 29% |
| Calendly | Shareable calendar link | Let others book time on your calendar | Eliminate back-and-forth coordination |
| Slack | Public channels | Give each of your teams a place to talk with each other | Break down silos |
| Fathom | Automatic meeting transcription | Get a written record of all of your calls | Never worry about forgetting something important |
| Notion | Linked databases | Connect docs, tables, and to-dos all in one platform | Make your team more efficient |

Short capability phrases in Mia's style: "auto-drafting invoices," "field-level provenance," "finance-approved process."

Rules:
- Messaging is structured capability first, then the proof point for each capability: Capability | What it does for the buyer | Proof | Buyer voice.
- A capability describes what the customer can now do, never what the company has.
  Bad: "the abusers Stripe already knows." Better: a phrase naming the screening the product performs.
- No abstract nouns as capabilities: visibility, insights, intelligence, protection, platform.
  Rewrite each one as the mechanism behind it:

  | Abstract | Concrete |
  |---|---|
  | Better visibility | Line-item payout matching |
  | Revenue intelligence | Auto-tagging deal risks |
  | Fraud protection | Pre-authorization card checks |

- Test: could an engineer point to the feature behind this capability?
- Mark any capability with no proof or no buyer quote as a gap, inside the table.

## 3. Hero line

### Step 1: know who reads the hero

| Visitor | What they read |
|---|---|
| Already knows the product | Goes straight to the CTA |
| Loosely familiar with the product | Reads the hero |
| Has heard of it but doesn't know what it is | Reads the whole page |

The hero is written for the loosely familiar visitor. The sections below it carry the capabilities and proof for everyone else.

### Step 2: choose the anchor with the decision tree

Answer each question in order, and state the answer and the evidence for it in one line each.

**Q1. Is the product category mature or immature?**
Mature means buyers already know the category and compare vendors within it. Immature means buyers are still learning the category exists.

**If mature, Q2. Are you differentiating on the product or the market?**
- **Market** (a specific audience the category ignores): **anchor on the persona.**
  Canva: "The design tool for people who aren't designers."
- **Product:** go to Q3.

**Q3. Are you differentiating on a single feature or a set of features?**
- **Set of features** (a different kind of product in a known category): **anchor on the category.**
  The Browser Company: "Get excited about your web browser."
- **One feature** (a single standout no one else has): **anchor on the feature.**
  Nokia: "1 phone. 5 cameras."

**If immature, Q2. Are you solving one big problem or a collection of small problems?**
- **Collection of small problems** (the product replaces a messy multi-step process): **anchor on the capability.**
  Uber: "Push a button, get a ride."
- **One big problem:** go to Q3.

**Q3. Which is more compelling: eliminating the problem, or the outcome that drives?**
- **Elimination** (the pain is vivid and widely felt): **anchor on the problem.**
  Loom: "Loom on. Meetings off."
- **Outcome** (the result is measurable and valuable): **anchor on the benefit.**
  Butter: "Increase top-line ARR by 5% by fixing payment failures."

| Anchor | Path | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Persona | Mature, market | Canva |
| Category | Mature, product, set of features | The Browser Company |
| Feature | Mature, product, one feature | Nokia |
| Capability | Immature, collection of small problems | Uber |
| Problem | Immature, one big problem, elimination | Loom |
| Benefit | Immature, one big problem, outcome | Butter |

If the evidence for a branch is thin, say so, and show the hero for both branches.

### Step 3: decide the frame, against or for

**Positioned against** a named alternative the buyer already uses:

| Against | Hero line |
|---|---|
| Email | Slack replaces email inside your company. |
| Meetings | Loom on. Meetings off. |
| Zoom | Zoom out. (Around) |

**Positioned for** an activity the buyer is trying to do:

| For | Hero line |
|---|---|
| Audience research | SparkToro shows who your audience is, what they talk about, and where to reach them. |
| Competitive intelligence | Compete like you mean it. (Crayon) |
| Software billing | Spend your time growing, not billing. |

Use "against" when buyers have a clear incumbent they already dislike. Use "for" when the buyer thinks in terms of a job, not a tool.

### Step 4: write and recommend

- Write 3 to 5 hero lines in the chosen anchor and frame.
- Give each a subhead that carries the specifics: the capabilities, a number, or the mechanism.
- Recommend one, and cite the research finding it rests on.

## Style notes and exceptions

- No em dashes.
- Avoid "X, not Y" constructions by default. The billing example above uses one deliberately, as a parallel against-frame. Use it only when the contrast is the whole point of the line, and never more than once per page.
