# Competitive intelligence modes

Read the relevant mode and use templates.md for evidence checks and outputs.

## Competitive landscape

**Job:** Establish a scoped, comparable alternative universe for a customer job.
Required: job/problem, comparison scope, intended use.
Optional: profiles, customer research, win/loss findings, buying criteria,
category analysis, and approved product facts. URLs are not required.

Answer which alternatives belong and why; which are observed shortlist competitors,
substitutes, services, internal builds, or status quo; supported differences;
and what coverage is insufficient. Relevance is scoped, not a global vendor label.

Discover from customer evidence, procurement/evaluation records, documentation,
and category sources. Use SEO overlap only as a discovery lead. Mark buyer-evidenced
versus hypothesized alternatives. List plausible unverified alternatives as
hypotheses, not confirmed shortlist members. Record relevance exclusions.
Reuse dossiers; request only fields needed to close comparison gaps.

Choose dimensions from the question and available buyer criteria. Operationalize
map axes and cite placements; never force a two-axis map on weak evidence.
Compare equivalent tiers/versions/dates/geographies and explain normalization.
Unknown capability is not absence; unsupported differences remain gaps.
An empty map quadrant is not proof of demand, advantage, or a positioning wedge.

Output: alternative register and inclusion rationale; comparable evidence matrix;
coverage/freshness; supported maps if useful; contradictions; research implications;
readiness; specialist return; ranked gaps. _summary.md holds this derived view.
Consumers: PMC, positioning, market entry, pricing, sales enablement, comparison pages.
Customer-research owns buyer-reported alternatives; win-loss owns outcome patterns.
Do not re-perform their interpretation or select our positioning/target market.

## Entity profiling

**Job:** Build or verify a source-linked dossier on one named alternative.
Required: identity and question or desired depth.
Optional: URLs, supplied dossiers, documentation, demos, reviews, prior profiles,
field evidence, and company context. A targeted request needs no full scrape.

Answer what it offers and claims, to whom, on what terms, which capabilities/limits
apply to a version/tier/region, and what remains unverified.
Use original product/pricing docs, releases, permitted demos/trials, original
filings, supplied research, and customer findings. Use SEO only if relevant.
Default to a focused profile when the question is narrow; otherwise quick scan.
A deep profile expands relevant coverage, not mandatory tool calls.

Quick scan: identity, observed market-facing framing, relevant capabilities,
commercial terms, provenance/verification, limitations, gaps.
Deep profile: add applicable integrations, implementation/security requirements,
customer evidence, releases, company facts, optional content/SEO, and comparative
implications only when our product facts and buyer relevance support them.

Maintain field-level states: claimed, documented, directly verified,
customer-reported, unknown, or explicitly absent within scope.
Keep availability (announced, beta, limited, generally available, withdrawn,
unknown) separate from verification. Documentation is not hands-on testing.
A review does not establish a universal limitation. Website omission is not absence.
Log what was tested, environment/version/date, and test limits when directly verified.
Commercial terms need currency, billing interval, tier, unit, region, effective
period, and exclusions; a changed starting price may reflect a different tier.

Output: focused/full dossier, sources/raw references, findings, history, opposing
evidence, readiness, specialist return, ranked gaps. Preserve useful existing
profile sections when their contents remain relevant and supported.
Consumers: landscape, signals, PMC, positioning, pricing, sales enablement, pages.
Do not decide our pricing or positioning, infer deal causes, or write collateral.

## Competitive signals

**Job:** Establish material external changes against dated baselines and route reviews.
Required: entity/watch scope and baseline or supplied event.
Optional: snapshots, watch dimensions, canon references, materiality criteria,
requested review interval. Without a baseline establish current state and mark
change unknown; never invent a previous value.

Answer what changed, event/effective date versus observation date, whether it is
announced/available/tested/rumored/retracted, what evidence verifies the delta,
and which findings/artifacts/decisions may need review.
Compare saved pages, release docs, terms, filings, announcements, and field inputs.
Deduplicate syndication. Verify changed fields before summarizing implications.
A page disappearance does not prove removal. Preserve corrections and retractions.

An announcement is not availability. Verify rollout, access, tier/region/version,
effective date, and documentation. Hiring/funding may suggest direction but never
prove intent or future delivery. Separate event confidence from impact confidence.
Assess materiality using affected scope, feasibility, claim accuracy, urgency,
and decision impact, not arbitrary event counts. One credible change may matter.

Output: event/delta register; prior/current references; verification/availability;
materiality; affected finding/profile/comparison/asset IDs; hypotheses and limits;
review requests; readiness; specialist return; gaps. _signals.md is optional.
Refresh affected fields and derived comparisons when saving is authorized.
Retain the old baseline and finding supersession links.
Consumers: profiling/landscape, PMC, market intelligence, pricing, positioning,
sales enablement, and SSOT review. SSOT alone proposes drift verdicts and handles
canon gates. Outcome effects belong to win-loss. Monitoring installation or
external alerts require their own authorization; a cadence is only a plan.
