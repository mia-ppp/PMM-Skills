# General customer research design and execution

Use for a consequential customer question or methodological support to a
specialist. Load `../../_shared/context-establishment.md` for handoff/return fields.
Keep domain interpretation with its owner. Pricing methods remain in
pricing-strategy; market selection, outcome coding and persona decisions retain
their existing owners. No approved persona or market is required to explore.

## Plan the smallest useful study

1. **Define the decision.** Reuse the goal and GAP IDs. State what changes if the
   answer differs, competing explanations, existing evidence and exclusions.
2. **Reuse before collecting.** Inspect available notes/data and missing internal
   facts. Explain why retrieval or re-analysis cannot answer the question.
3. **Select a method.** Interviews explore jobs, behavior and reasons; contextual
   observation tests actual workflows; surveys estimate distributions only with
   an appropriate sampling frame. Online sources discover language/hypotheses,
   not representative prevalence. Triangulate stated behavior with records when
   useful. The domain owner chooses specialist methods and interpretation.
4. **Define the sample.** Specify population, inclusion/exclusion, segment/role/
   stage variation, independent accounts, negative cases and likely bias. State
   recruiting channels, availability, suggested effort/time and constraints.
   Do not treat five reviews as equivalent to five interviews or invent precision.
5. **Prepare recruitment and instruments.** Provide a neutral invitation and
   screener for role, relevant recent experience and decision involvement.
   Avoid screening only for satisfied advocates. Write an interview guide around
   a recent concrete episode, trigger, workflow, alternatives, stakes, evaluation,
   decision and outcome. Probe examples before opinions; avoid pitching or leading.
   Pilot survey wording and distinguish open answers from prompted options.
6. **Plan analysis and sufficiency.** Define coding, contextual splits, duplicate
   handling, supporting/negative evidence and denominator rules before collection.
   Name the observations that would support or challenge each hypothesis and the
   coverage needed for this decision. Stop when the question is adequately
   answered within scope, or report inconclusive at the time/resource limit.
   Qualitative saturation is scoped judgment, not a statistical proof or count.

## Guide execution without pretending it happened

- **Separate plan from collected evidence.** Track planned, collected, inspected
  and analyzed sources. Empty recruitment or interview tables are not findings.
- **Respect action scope.** Draft instruments and guide the PMM's collection;
  contact people, spend money or access systems only with applicable authorization.
- **Capture provenance.** Record participant/source role, date, segment/stage,
  exact quote locators, method, consent/use restrictions where supplied and bias.
  Keep source reports, observed behavior and researcher interpretation separate.
- **Synthesize all relevant evidence.** Code themes and negative cases; separate
  context differences from genuine conflicts. Do not select only vivid quotations
  or equate repeated documents with independent participants.
- **Explain confidence.** Consider applicability, independence, coverage, method,
  recency/product version, bias and opposing evidence. A narrow segment pattern
  need not recur in unrelated segments; it cannot be generalized to them.
- **Return usable findings.** Use the shared research-return contract, assess each
  completion criterion, and identify follow-up or stopping conditions. Keep source
  language, interpretation and proposed marketing language distinct.

End with decision-specific PROCEED, PROCEED PROVISIONALLY or HOLD THIS DECISION,
its rationale, and the ranked evidence gaps. Approval dependencies remain separate.
When evidence is incomplete, provide a bounded assumption/risk/validation trigger
or hold only the consequential choice. Continue unrelated analysis.
