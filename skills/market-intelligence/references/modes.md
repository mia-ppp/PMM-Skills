# Market intelligence modes

Read only the relevant mode. All use the evidence/return contract and the domain
requirements in research-return.md.

## Market overview

**Job:** Explain a defined market's structure, demand conditions, economic drivers,
constraints, and changes without deciding whether the company should enter.

Required: research question/intended use, market/problem scope, geography, time
horizon. Unknown boundaries may be explicit provisional hypotheses.
Optional: product context, approved decisions, reports, datasets, historical
research, customer findings, and competitive artifacts.

Answer what is in/out of scope, how sources define it, how supply/buying/distribution
works, what adoption/purchase/spending evidence exists, which technology/economic/
ecosystem/regulatory changes matter, and what remains disputed.

Use official statistics, original studies, filings, industry bodies, procurement
records, technical developments, and authoritative regulatory sources.
Analyst estimates need definitions, methods, period, and limitations. Trace reports
citing one study to the underlying study; do not count them independently.
Request primary customer validation from customer-research; retain market synthesis.

Distinguish search interest, expressed interest, usage, purchase, and spending.
Attention is not demand; correlation is not causality. Reconcile stocks/flows,
revenue/transaction volume, currencies, nominal/real values, geography, periods,
and overlapping segments. Show formulas and ranges for supported derivations.
Never add incomparable totals. Supply sizing anchors, not company-specific
TAM/SAM/SOM, segment attractiveness scores, or entry rankings.

Output: boundary definition; structure/value chain; demand/constraint findings;
dated drivers/trends; metric reconciliation; competing explanations; research
implications; readiness and specialist return; ranked gaps.
Consumers: PMC, market entry, ICP/personas, positioning, pricing, and GTM.

## Category analysis

**Job:** Describe how solution categories are defined, named, discovered, compared,
and evaluated, including changing conventions.

Required: problem/use case or candidate category, research question.
Optional: buyer language, search data, analyst taxonomies, directory classifications,
competitor profiles, and current approved positioning.

Answer which labels buyers/vendors/analysts/platforms use, boundaries and disputed
membership, evaluation expectations, and evidence of convergence or fragmentation.
Compare original taxonomies, published evaluation criteria, customer-language
findings, search behavior, documentation, and historical definitions.

Separate buyer usage, vendor claims, analyst classification, and platform labels.
Directory classification does not establish buyer understanding. Vendor relabeling
alone does not prove convergence or buyer adoption. Table-stakes claims require
scoped evaluation evidence, not repeated feature lists. Reuse exact customer
language through customer-research's language bank.

Output: definitions/inclusion boundaries; terminology-by-source-role map;
evaluation expectations; convergence/split evidence; unresolved definitions;
implications/tests; readiness and specialist return; ranked gaps.
Consumers: PMC, positioning, market overview, landscape, market entry, messaging.

Do not choose our category, recommend category creation, reposition, or write
message architecture. Route company-frame choices to positioning-strategy.
Keep current approved framing intact even when external vocabulary changes.

## Emerging opportunity investigation

**Job:** Investigate testable opportunities arising from changed conditions and
unmet-need evidence. Produce hypotheses, not market or investment choices.

Required: scoped opportunity question or market/category findings to investigate.
Optional: customer findings, competitive changes, product constraints, business
goals, prior hypotheses, and experiment results.

Answer what changed, who appears affected, how needs might change, what supports
or opposes the hypothesis, which alternative explanation fits, and what smallest
useful test would distinguish them. Reuse findings and source IDs. Seek negative
cases, historical analogues, scope exceptions, and temporary-attention explanations.

Whitespace is not proof of unmet demand or company fit. A rule change does not
prove customers will buy. A feature gap does not establish what we should ship.
Keep need, urgency, willingness to pay, fit, and ability to win distinct.

Output a hypothesis register:

| ID | Hypothesis/change mechanism | Affected population | Supporting/opposing FND IDs | Assumptions/limits | Observation/implication confidence | Smallest test/completion criteria | Owner | Status/review trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use untested, investigating, supported within scope, inconclusive, or not supported
within scope. Preserve superseded hypotheses and reasons. Rank investigation
priority by materiality, uncertainty, urgency, and resolution effort. Do not
convert it into segment attractiveness, market selection, or investment priority.
Attach readiness and specialist return; end with ranked gaps.
Consumers: PMC, market entry, ICP/personas, pricing, product, experimentation.
These owners evaluate strategic choices and commitments. Research authorizes neither.
