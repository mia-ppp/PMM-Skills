### 1. Why Small Teams Look for Asana Alternatives

If you're a team of 5–20 people searching for an Asana alternative, you've probably hit one of two walls:

**Price.** Asana's free plan runs out fast, and the tiers that unlock the features you actually need — timeline view, workload management, advanced automation — push you into $24.99/user/month territory. For a 15-person team, that's over $4,500/year just for project management.

**Complexity.** Asana was built to handle enterprise-scale, cross-functional work. That means portfolios, goals, custom fields, workload charts, and admin controls most small teams never touch — but still have to navigate around. Reviewers consistently mention a steep ramp-up period and "feature fatigue": paying for and configuring around capability you don't use.

Small teams don't need an operating system for work. They need a place to see what's due, who's doing it, and whether it's on track — without a two-week onboarding process.

---

### 2. What to Look for in an Asana Alternative

Before you pick, evaluate options against what actually matters for a 5–20 person team:

- **Time to value** — Can your team be productive on day one, or does it need a dedicated admin to configure?
- **Price at your actual size** — Not the marketing price. What does it cost for your team size, on the tier with the features you need?
- **Feature-to-complexity ratio** — Does the tool give you what you need without forcing you to wade through what you don't?
- **Room to grow** — Will it still work at 30 or 50 people, or will you outgrow it in a year?
- **Support quality** — Small teams don't have time to wait days for a support ticket.
