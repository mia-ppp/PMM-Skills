# A-25

## Prompt

i need copy for my pricing page. we have three plans: starter ($29/mo), pro ($79/mo), business ($199/mo). it's a social media scheduling tool for marketers

## Output

Before I draft this, I need a bit more context to make the copy actually persuasive rather than generic. Here's what I have vs. what I need:

**Have:** 3 plans, pricing ($29/$79/$199), social media scheduling tool, marketers as audience.

**Need:**

1. **Plan differentiation** — What actually changes between tiers? (e.g., # of social accounts, # of scheduled posts, team seats, analytics depth, approval workflows, AI captions, etc.) I need the real feature deltas to make each plan sound distinct rather than "more of the same."

2. **Audience segment per plan** — Is this roughly:
   - Starter = solo marketer / freelancer
   - Pro = small marketing team / agency
   - Business = larger team / multiple clients or brands
   
   Or is it split some other way (e.g., by company size, not team size)?

3. **Recommended plan** — Which one do you want most people to land on? (Usually the middle tier gets visual emphasis — confirming that's Pro.)

4. **Proof points** — Any real numbers/testimonials/logos I can use? (e.g., "10,000 marketers," "trusted by X agency," star rating) — I won't fabricate these, so tell me what's true or I'll leave placeholders.

5. **Key objections** — What makes people hesitate before buying? Common ones for this category:
   - "Will it integrate with [platforms]?"
   - "What if I need to cancel / switch plans?"
   - "Is there a free trial?"
   
   Let me know which apply.

6. **Trial/guarantee** — Free trial? Money-back guarantee? Annual discount? This affects the CTA and risk-reversal copy.

7. **Traffic source** — Are people landing here cold (from ads/SEO) or already warm (came from homepage, know the product)? Changes how much re-explaining the page needs to do.

Send me what you've got (even rough notes are fine) and I'll write the full page — headlines, plan cards, feature comparison copy, FAQ/objection handling, and CTAs.
