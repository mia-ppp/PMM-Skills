# A-18

## Prompt

Our pricing page has three tiers but nobody picks the middle one. 60% choose the cheapest plan and 30% bounce entirely. What should we change?

## Output

# Diagnosing Your Pricing Page

Before prescribing fixes, let's identify what's actually happening. The symptom (nobody picks the middle tier) usually traces back to one of these root causes:

## Likely Culprits

**1. The decoy isn't decoying**
The middle tier's whole job is to make the top tier look reasonable (or make itself look obviously best). If 60% go cheapest, your middle plan probably isn't positioned as the "smart default" — it's just... a plan.

**2. Value gap, not price gap**
If people default to cheapest, they may not understand *what they lose* by skipping the middle tier. The differentiation is probably feature-list clutter instead of outcome-based framing.

**3. The 30% bounce rate is the bigger fire**
That's likely a separate problem — probably page clarity, load speed, or unclear value prop before price is even parsed. Fixing the tier psychology won't matter if 3 in 10 people leave before evaluating anything.

## What I'd Test, in Order

**Fix the bounce first**
- Above-the-fold: one sentence answering "what do I get and why should I care" before showing prices
- Check page speed / mobile rendering — 30% bounce is high enough to suspect a technical or clarity issue, not just pricing psychology

**Then fix tier framing**
- Make the middle tier visually "the plan" — badge it ("Most Popular," "Best Value"), give it a highlighted border/background
- Anchor high: consider whether your *top* tier is priced to make middle look cheap by comparison (classic decoy effect)
- Rename tiers around outcomes, not size ("Starter / Growth / Scale" > "Basic / Pro / Enterprise")

**Then fix the value ladder**
- List 3–4 killer features unique to middle tier, not shared with cheapest — right now the delta probably feels marginal
- Add a "what you're missing" microcopy under the cheap tier ("No priority support" etc.) so opting out feels like a real tradeoff

## Quick Diagnostic Questions
- Is the middle tier priced closer to cheap or closer to premium? (should feel closer to premium value, cheap-ish price)
- Do people bounce *before* or *after* seeing pricing? (session recordings would tell you fast)
- What's the price *ratio* between tiers? (2x jumps feel safer to skip than 1.3x jumps)
