# A-16

## Prompt

Our positioning is 'the all-in-one workspace for modern teams.' We keep losing deals to Notion and Linear. Rework it.

## Output

# The Problem with Your Current Positioning

"All-in-one workspace for modern teams" is a description, not a position. It tells me what category you're in, not why I should pick you over the two products you just named. It also has a fatal flaw: it's trying to be everything to everyone, which is exactly what makes you vulnerable to specialists.

Here's the deeper issue — **Notion and Linear didn't win by being all-in-one.** Notion won by being the best flexible workspace for docs/wikis/knowledge. Linear won by being the fastest, most opinionated tool for engineering teams tracking issues. They both picked a fight and won it. "All-in-one" tries to avoid picking a fight, which reads as generic in a competitive eval.

## Questions before I give you real alternatives:

1. **Who do you actually win with today?** (industry, team size, function — not aspirational, actual closed-won deals)
2. **What's the #1 reason those winners chose you over Notion/Linear?** (Was it price, a specific feature, integration, support, something structural?)
3. **What's the #1 reason you lose?** (Feature gap? Brand trust? They already had one installed?)

## In the meantime, here's the reframe framework I'd use:

Instead of positioning by *breadth* (all-in-one), position by a **specific wedge** — a job Notion and Linear are structurally bad at.

Some candidate directions (pick based on your real differentiation):

- **"The workspace where planning and execution are the same tool"** — if your edge is that Notion is docs-first (weak on structured work/tracking) and Linear is issues-first (weak on docs/knowledge), you sit in the gap.
- **"Built for teams who outgrew Notion but aren't just engineers"** — positions you as the graduation path, targeting ops/cross-functional teams who find Linear too dev-centric and Notion too unstructured at scale.
- **"One source of truth, not five tabs open"** — if switching cost/context-fragmentation is your actual pain point you solve, lean into that pain, not the feature list.

## The actual work:
Send me the answers to those 3 questions and I'll draft:
- A tight positioning statement (For [X], who [Y], [Product] is the [category] that [benefit], unlike [Notion/Linear], we [reason to believe]).
- 2-3 messaging variants to test in sales calls this week (positioning should be tested against real objections before it goes on your homepage).

What's your actual wedge?
