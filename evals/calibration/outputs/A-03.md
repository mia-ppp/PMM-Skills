# A-03

## Prompt

Our messaging pillars are: Easy to use, Secure, Scalable, Great support. Fix them. We're a B2B data pipeline tool competing with Fivetran.

## Output

# Messaging Pillars: Diagnosis & Fix

Your current pillars have a real problem: **they're generic enough to apply to literally any B2B SaaS product** — including Fivetran. If your competitor could say the exact same four words about themselves, they're not doing any work for you.

## What's wrong with each one

**"Easy to use"** — Fivetran's entire brand is built on this. You cannot out-position the market leader on their home turf with the same claim. This pillar actively helps them.

**"Secure"** — Table stakes. Nobody buys a data pipeline tool that admits to being insecure. Saying "secure" is like a restaurant advertising "food." It's not a differentiator, it's a hygiene factor.

**"Scalable"** — Meaningless without a number, a use case, or a contrast. Scalable compared to what? At what volume? For whom?

**"Great support"** — Everyone claims this. It's unfalsifiable until a prospect is already a customer, so it does zero work in the consideration phase.

## The real fix: find your wedge against Fivetran specifically

Generic pillars come from not answering: *why do people choose you INSTEAD of Fivetran?* Before I rewrite these, I need you to answer (or let me propose hypotheses to react to):

1. **Where do you actually win deals?** Price? A connector Fivetran doesn't have? Self-hosted/on-prem option? Row-based pricing vs. their consumption pricing?
2. **Who complains about Fivetran that becomes your customer?** (Common ones: unpredictable/expensive MAR-based pricing, black-box transformations, support that's slow at scale, limited customization)
3. **What can you do that requires engineering effort Fivetran won't make?** (Custom connectors, on-prem/VPC deployment, open-source core, etc.)

## Draft alternative pillars (pick your fights)

If your wedge is **pricing predictability**:
- "Transparent, predictable pricing" (vs. Fivetran's MAR surprise-bill reputation)

If your wedge is **control/customization**:
- "Built for engineers who need control" (vs. Fivetran's black-box abstraction)

If your wedge is **deployment flexibility**:
- "Runs in your VPC, not just ours" (if true — security/compliance teams care a lot)

If your wedge is **connector coverage/speed**:
- "New connectors in days, not quarters" (if you can back this up)

## What I need from you
Tell me which of these is actually true for your product, or give me your win/loss notes, and I'll turn it into a tight, defensible pillar set with proof points for each — the kind that makes a Fivetran evaluator pause instead of defaulting to the category leader.
