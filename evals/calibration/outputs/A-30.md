# A-30

## Prompt

Our pricing page has three tiers but nobody picks the middle one. 60% choose the cheapest plan and 30% bounce entirely. What should we change?

## Output

Quick note: I don't have a `product-marketing-context.md` in this session, so I'll ask a few clarifying questions but give you a full diagnosis based on the pattern you're describing — it's a very common one.

## Diagnosis

Your numbers tell a specific story:

- **60% picking cheapest** → your tiers aren't differentiated enough to justify the price jump, or the middle tier isn't visually/psychologically positioned as the "smart choice." This is usually an anchoring failure, not a pricing failure.
- **30% bouncing** → either sticker shock (price shown before value is established), unclear value prop for who this is even for, or decision paralysis (three similar-looking options with no guidance).

Combined, this looks less like "our prices are wrong" and more like **the page isn't doing the psychological work of steering the decision.**

## Task-Specific Questions (before we finalize)

1. What's the price gap between tiers? (e.g., $10 / $30 / $80 — proportional jumps matter a lot here)
2. Is the cheapest tier actually good enough to solve the core use case? If so, that's your real problem.
3. Where's this traffic coming from — cold or already product-qualified?
4. Do you have session recordings on this page? I'd want to see if people are comparing tiers or just going straight to the cheapest CTA.
5. What have you already tried?

## Quick Wins (Implement Now)

1. **Visually anchor the middle tier** — badge ("Most Popular" / "Recommended"), slightly larger card, contrasting color, positioned dead center. If you're not doing this already, it's the single highest-leverage fix.
2. **Make the cheapest plan's limitations obvious** — if it currently looks "basically fine," people will never upgrade. List what's *missing*, not just what's included.
3. **Reorder features so the differentiator is the first bullet under the middle tier** — don't bury the reason to upgrade.
4. **Add a one-line "who this is for" under each tier name** — reduces the "which plan is right for me?" anxiety that's likely driving both bad behaviors.
5. **Check that CTA copy differs by tier** — "Start Free Trial" (cheap tier) vs. "Start Free Trial — Most Popular" or similar, so the CTA itself carries the nudge.

## High-Impact Changes (Prioritize)

1. **Re-architect the decoy structure.** Classic pattern: cheapest tier should be *just* limited enough that a meaningful chunk of your audience can't use it seriously (too few seats, no key integration, hard usage cap). Right now it sounds like it's fully viable, which is why 60% stop there. Decide who cheap tier should *not* satisfy, and cut features accordingly.
2. **Reconsider price proportionality.** If middle tier is priced too close to cheap, there's no anchor effect. If it's priced too close to the top tier, it disappears. Middle should feel like "clearly more value for a bit more money" — not the safe middle ground of a range.
3. **Add value before price.** If price is visible above the fold before any benefit/outcome framing, that's likely contributing to your 30% bounce. Add a short value-reinforcing section (outcome-based headline + 1-line proof) before the tier grid, even on a pricing page.
4. **Add social proof next to the middle tier specifically.** A logo strip or "X% of customers choose this plan" stat next to the middle option is one of the most reliable nudges in pricing page CRO.
5. **Add an FAQ/objection section below the tiers** addressing "what if I outgrow this," "can I switch plans," "what happens after the trial" — this targets your bounce, not just tier selection.

## Test Ideas (A/B Test, Don't Assume)

- Test middle tier **highlighted vs. not** — isolate the anchoring effect specifically.
- Test **removing a feature from the cheap tier** that currently makes it "good enough."
- Test **price framing**: monthly vs. annual default, or "per seat" vs. "flat" — this can shift which tier feels rational.
- Test **adding a 4th "Enterprise/Contact Us" tier** — this can make the top paid tier feel more like "the real top choice" and pull people up from cheap into middle.
- Test **value-first pricing page layout** (benefit statement + proof above the fold, pricing table below) vs. current price-first layout, to isolate whether bounce is price shock or unclear value.

## Copy Alternatives (Middle Tier CTA)

- Current (assumed): "Get Started" — weak, no differentiation
- Alt 1: **"Start Free Trial — Most Teams Choose This"** — leans into social proof
- Alt 2: **"Start with [Tier Name] →"** — personalizes and reduces generic feel
- Alt 3: **"Get [specific outcome], Free for 14 Days"** — outcome-focused, reduces price anxiety by leading with trial
